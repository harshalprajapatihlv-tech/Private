A. Last 30 minutes — what to actually look at

  1. Equation: Assets = Liabilities + Capital. Dual aspect: two effects every time.
  2. Debit increases assets, expenses, drawings. Credit increases liabilities, capital, incomes.
  3. Journal skeleton in your head: Dr. … To … (Being …).
  4. FIFO: old stock leaves first; newest remains. WAM: mix the pool after every purchase; issue at the mix.
  5. SLM: (Cost − Scrap) / Life, equal each year. WDV: Book value × rate, falling each year.
  6. Part-year: months of use / 12. Indian year-end often 31 March.
  7. Schedule III P&L heads: Revenue from operations, Other income, Expenses (purchases, change in inventory, employee, finance, depreciation, other), Tax, Profit.
  8. Schedule III BS heads: Shareholders’ funds, Non-current liabilities, Current liabilities; Non-current assets, Current assets.
  9. CFS add-backs: depreciation, losses on sale, interest expense (then show interest paid in financing). Deduct: profit on sale, income moved to investing.
  10. WC rule: increase in current assets = cash went out. Increase in current liabilities = cash was held back.
  11. Twelve ratios: Current, Quick, Debt–equity, Proprietary, GP%, NP%, Operating ratio, Inventory turnover, Debtors days, ROCE, ROE, Interest coverage.
  12. Identification: last sentence of the question = the requirement.

Panic protocol (when a numerical looks ugly)

  1. Write the format / skeleton immediately. Formats score.
  2. Fill every figure you are sure of. Leave a blank with a working-note number for the rest.
  3. Open a working note: ‘WN-1 Depreciation’, ‘WN-2 Closing stock’. Examiners read workings.
  4. Check one identity: units (inventory), totals (TB, BS), or cash movement (CFS).
  5. If two methods are possible, pick one, label it, finish it. An incomplete perfect method scores less than a complete labelled one.
  6. Never leave a theory question blank. Dump the definition, three reasons, one example.

B. Final quick revision — chapter recap

Bootcamp

Accounting records and explains business money-events so owners can see profit, position, and cash. A transaction changes value and has evidence. Assets are what the business owns; liabilities are what it owes; capital is the owner’s residual claim. Revenue increases that claim; expenses decrease it; drawings are the owner taking value out. Debit is left; credit is right. Cash coming in is a debit to Cash.

Chapter 1

Concepts (entity, money measurement, going concern, cost, dual aspect, accrual, matching, prudence, consistency, materiality, disclosure, periodicity) tell you how to record. Journal is date-wise; ledger is account-wise; trial balance tests arithmetic equality of debits and credits. Flow: voucher → journal → ledger → trial balance → financial statements.

Chapter 2

Closing stock is a current asset and it changes COGS, so it changes profit. FIFO assumes oldest goods are sold first. WAM uses a weighted mix. Always reconcile units. AS-2: inventory at cost or NRV, whichever is lower. LIFO is not used in India.

Chapter 3

Depreciation allocates cost over useful life. It is not cash leaving. SLM = equal yearly amount. WDV = reducing amount. Add installation to cost. Charge till the date of sale. Profit/loss on sale = proceeds versus NBV.

Chapter 4

Companies present Statement of P&L and Balance Sheet as per Schedule III. Notes complete the story. Adjustments implement accrual: outstanding, prepaid, depreciation, provisions. The Balance Sheet must tie. A company has share capital and reserves, not ‘capital + drawings’.

Chapter 5

Profit is an accrual story; cash flow is a wallet story. Operating = running the shop (indirect: profit, add non-cash, squeeze WC, pay tax). Investing = buying/selling long tools and investments. Financing = owners and lenders. A+B+C must explain the change in cash.

Chapter 6

The annual report is the year’s public file: narrative (MD&A, Directors’ Report), governance, auditor’s opinion, and the financial statements with notes. Read the auditor first, then statements, then notes, then the story. Users: owners, lenders, analysts, employees, government.

Chapter 7

Ratios turn statements into questions: can we pay bills (liquidity), can we survive debt (solvency), do we earn (profitability), do we use assets (turnover)? Balance sheet ratios use only BS; revenue ratios only P&L; combined ratios mix both. Write formula, substitute, unit, one-line meaning.

Memory tricks — last look

  • DEAD CLIC: Debit Expenses, Assets, Drawings; Credit Liabilities, Income, Capital.
  • FIFO: first in, first out — the new stock is still on the shelf.
  • WAM: stir the soup, then ladle. Do not stir when you ladle (issue).
  • SLM: same load every year. WDV: the new phone loses more value in year one.
  • Outstanding = still owed = liability. Prepaid = paid too early = asset.
  • Depreciation: add back in cash flow, because you never paid it in cash this year.
  • Increase in debtors: you made sales but the cash is still in the customer’s pocket.
  • ROCE uses EBIT and all long-term funds. ROE uses PAT and owners’ funds only.
  • Schedule III Balance Sheet: who funded it (equity + liabilities) = what we hold (assets).
Exam tip

When time is almost up, stop hunting for a perfect last figure. Box the answer you have, write ‘WN’ for anything unfinished, and move to a theory question. Theory in complete sentences still scores.
